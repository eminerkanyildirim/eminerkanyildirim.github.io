About

Electronic engineer working end-to-end: the board, the firmware on it, and the software that makes sense of what it measures.

I'm an electronic engineer who likes owning a problem across the whole stack — designing the PCB, writing the firmware that runs on it, and building the software that reads and visualises its data. That means a signal-integrity decision on the board, an I2C setup in firmware, and a plotting loop in the desktop app are all part of the same job.

Day to day I work in an appliance R&D lab, verifying electronic control boards for functionality, performance and IEC 60335 safety compliance — writing the requirements, running the benchmarks, and making the calls that keep a product safe and certifiable. Alongside that, my project work sits where analog meets digital: mixed-signal boards, low-power wireless (BLE, LoRa), real-time telemetry, and the DSP that cleans up messy signals. Earlier on I helped run the electronics for a university solar-race team and interned across embedded software and automotive CAN systems.

How I work

  • Start from the real constraint — power budget, timing, cost, compliance — not the tool.
  • Prototype early, measure, and let the data settle the argument.
  • Prefer the simplest design that works: fewer parts, fewer failure modes.
  • Write firmware and tooling that the next person can actually read.
